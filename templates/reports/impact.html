York County Library

Community Impact Report

FY{{ fy1 }} & FY{{ fy2 }} Statistical Highlights  ·  Survey Insights: Strategic Plan Public Survey, April 2026 (n = 544)

{# ── Intro ── #}

In spring 2026, York County Library asked the community what it values most. 544 patrons responded — and their answers affirm what our data has shown for years: YCL is a vital, deeply used community institution. This report pairs those survey findings with two full fiscal years of library statistics to tell the story of how York County uses its library.

Survey respondents skew toward existing library users (92.3% used YCL in the past year) and the 41–64 age group. Statistics cover FY{{ fy1 }} (Jul {{ fy1 - 1 }}–Jun {{ fy1 }}) and FY{{ fy2 }} (Jul {{ fy2 - 1 }}–Jun {{ fy2 }}).

{# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #} {# 1. CIRCULATION #} {# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #}
Circulation

Patrons love checking out books — the numbers back it up

In our patron survey, borrowing books was rated the most important library service by every single age group — achieving the highest scores across all four service categories (avg. 3.79–4.00 out of 5). It is the bedrock of everything we do.

Patrons also highlighted frustration with hold waitlists and small in-branch collections — particularly at Fort Mill — signaling that demand outpaces current capacity. One patron wrote, "It is hard to browse books as the selection is small in person." Our circulation numbers reflect a community eager to read.

{# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #} {# 2. GATE COUNT #} {# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #}
Library Visits

People are coming through the door — and coming back

92.3% of survey respondents visited YCL in the past year. Teens (12–18) are the most frequent visitors, with 57.1% coming weekly. Adults 25–40 follow closely at 49.7% weekly. Even seniors (65+) visit at 36–41% weekly — making YCL one of the most consistently used public institutions in the county.

The survey also identified a scheduling gap for non-users: the primary barrier to visiting is simply being too busy (17 of 42 non-users cited this) — a convenience issue, not a service-quality problem.

{# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #} {# 3. PROGRAMMING #} {# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #}
Programs & Events

Demand for programs far exceeds what our spaces allow

Children's programming is the most-requested service category in our survey — with respondents emphasizing not that programs don't exist, but that they fill up immediately. Working parents repeatedly asked for evening and weekend storytime. A tween gap (ages 8–12) was independently identified by multiple respondents as a programming blind spot.

Beyond children's programs, working adults in their 30s–50s asked for evening book clubs, craft classes, and adult education. "Why are there no weekend clubs?" was a sentiment repeated across dozens of responses.

61.2% of respondents have attended at least one YCL signature event — with the Summer Learning Challenge (272 selections) and Winter Reading Challenge (218) leading all events.

{# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #} {# 4. HOTSPOTS #} {# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #}
Digital Access & Hotspots

Connectivity is a core library service — and demand is growing

Internet access and Wi-Fi ranked as the top technology service in the survey, leading across the 41+ age groups (avg. 3.35–3.93). Safe gathering space — the other top technology service — was the priority for teens and 25–40 year-olds, where Wi-Fi access and physical presence go hand-in-hand.

YCL's hotspot lending program extends that connectivity into patrons' homes — a critical equity service for families without reliable broadband access. Digital adoption is broad: 50–64% of every age group uses the YCL app, including 59.3% of seniors — proving that digital-first services work across generations.

{# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #} {# 5. NEW LIBRARY CARDS #} {# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #}
New Library Cards

Every new card is a new member of the YCL community

New library card registrations reflect YCL's reach into the community — each card a first step for a patron toward books, programs, digital resources, and more. York County's population has grown dramatically in recent years, and the survey reflects this: respondents from every age group were active card holders, and the survey itself had strong representation from areas experiencing the most growth.

Survey respondents also noted that word of mouth is the strongest non-website channel for teens and young adults — meaning new patrons are often brought in by existing ones. Reaching non-users, who cited "too busy" as their primary barrier, remains the key challenge for future card growth.

{# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #} {# 6. PRINTS #} {# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #}
Printing & Computer Access

Public technology access remains an essential equity service

Public computer use was identified in the survey as a valued technology service, and printing remains a concrete, high-need service for patrons without home office access — covering job applications, government forms, school materials, and more. The survey's high rate of website adoption (86–93% across all age groups) and strong app usage show that digital literacy is broadly present in our community.

Survey respondents asked for more technology literacy programming — particularly AI tools, computer basics, and cybersecurity for seniors — signaling that the demand for tech-adjacent library services is growing beyond basic printing.
