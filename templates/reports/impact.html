{# ── Section 1: You Keep Coming Back ── #}
You Keep Coming Back

The library recorded {{ fmt(data.gate.v2) }} visits in FY{{ fy2 }}. Our survey confirms the pattern: 92.3% of respondents had visited in the past year, with teens leading the way — 57.1% visit every week, and nearly half of 25–40 year-olds (49.7%) do the same.

{{ fmt(data.pc_reservations.v2) }} PC reservation sessions show that for many patrons, the library is their primary point of internet and computer access — a function that survey respondents consistently rated among our most valued services.

Even patrons who visit less frequently stay connected: 86–93% use our website across all age groups, and 50–64% use the YCL mobile app — including 59% of seniors aged 65 and older.

Western York County needs another library. It doesn't have to have all the programs… but a location with computers, books, and a hold shelf so people in Hickory Grove don't have to plan their trips based on when they're running to York.
— Survey respondent

{# ── Section 3: Learning Together ── #}
Learning Together

YCL offered {{ fmt(data.sessions.v2) }} program sessions in FY{{ fy2 }}, drawing {{ fmt(data.attendance.v2) }} participants. Our survey found that 61.2% of patrons attended at least one YCL signature event, with teens and young adults leading at 71.4%.

Signature events were a particular strength: the Summer Learning Challenge received 272 selections and the Winter Reading Challenge 218 — showing that structured reading programs resonate across age groups.

Lifelong learning programs topped the list of what patrons want more of, chosen by 53% of respondents (288 selections). But a clear barrier emerged: the majority of employed adults simply cannot attend programs held during working hours. Evening (after 5 p.m.) and Saturday programming were among the most-requested changes.

Equity note: Fort Mill-only patrons attended signature events at a rate of 51.6% — nearly 20 points below Rock Hill patrons (71.5%). Capacity and space constraints at Fort Mill are a key driver of this gap.
The majority of the programs I am interested in are held during working hours. I'm only in my 40s and work full time but would love to connect with other people through these clubs — and it's just not possible during the week. Why are there no weekend clubs?
— Survey respondent
More events for kids aged 8–13. More science programs. I would love to see more Tween programming — my 10-year-old feels stuck between little kid programs and teen programs.
— Survey respondent (composite)
{# ── Section 4: Growing Our Community ── #}
Growing Our Community
Events for 20–30 somethings looking to make friends. Young adult activities (18–28)? More programs for the 18–22 college age range.
— Survey respondents

YCL issued {{ fmt(data.cards.v2) }} new library cards in FY{{ fy2 }}. New cardholders represent fresh connections to the community — and an opportunity to retain them through the services they value most.

Our survey skews toward established users (92.3% had visited in the past year), which means the 7.7% of respondents who are non-users or lapsed visitors are a window into who we're not yet reaching. Among non-users, 17 of 42 cited being too busy — pointing again to scheduling and convenience as the primary barrier to broader engagement.

Young adults aged 19–24 show the most untapped potential: only 15.4% visit weekly (vs. 57.1% of teens), suggesting that the transition out of school-age programming leaves a gap the library can fill with targeted young adult services.

{# ── Section 5: Expanding Access ── #}
Expanding Access Beyond Our Walls

{{ fmt(data.hotspots.v2) }} hotspot checkouts put internet access in the hands of patrons who need it most — at home, at work, and in transit. For many families, a YCL hotspot is the difference between connected and left behind.

In-branch, {{ fmt(data.pc_reservations.v2) }} PC reservation sessions reflect the library's role as a technology access point. Help from librarians — rated 3.62 to 3.93 out of 5 across all age groups — is the trusted guide that makes that access meaningful.

Having a tool rental/makerspace or woodshop area would be incredible! The Richland library in Columbia has a great makerspace that creates accessibility for a lot of people.
— Survey respondent
