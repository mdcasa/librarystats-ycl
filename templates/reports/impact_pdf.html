What the Numbers Mean — Community Voice

York County Library System  |  FY 2023–24
You Keep Coming Back

The library recorded {{ fmt(data.gate.v2) }} visits in FY24. Our survey confirms the pattern: 92.3% of respondents visited in the past year, with teens leading — 57.1% visit every week.

{{ fmt(data.pc_reservations.v2) }} PC reservation sessions show the library remains a critical technology access point. Website use runs 86–93% across all age groups; 50–64% use the YCL app, including 59% of seniors.

Western York County needs another library. It doesn't have to have all the programs… but a location with computers, books, and a hold shelf so people in Hickory Grove don't have to plan their trips based on when they're running to York.
— Survey respondent
Connecting You to Stories & Ideas

Borrowing books is the single highest-rated service across every age group (average 3.79–4.00 / 5). The numbers reflect that demand:

Patron feedback highlights growth areas: more physical copies at smaller branches, complete series in digital collections, and shorter Libby/Hoopla hold times.

It is hard to browse books as the selection is small in person. I do appreciate being able to get them online, but I love spontaneously getting books.
— Survey respondent
Learning Together

YCL offered {{ fmt(data.sessions.v2) }} program sessions, drawing {{ fmt(data.attendance.v2) }} participants. 61.2% of survey respondents attended a signature event — teens and young adults at 71.4%. Summer Learning Challenge (272 selections) and Winter Reading Challenge (218) led attendance.

Lifelong learning programs were the top strategic priority (53% of respondents), but a clear barrier remains: employed adults need programming after 5 p.m. and on Saturdays.

Equity note: Fort Mill event attendance (51.6%) trails Rock Hill (71.5%) by nearly 20 points — a measurable consequence of space constraints at the Fort Mill branch.
The majority of the programs I'm interested in are held during working hours. I'm only in my 40s and work full time but would love to connect with other people — and it's just not possible during the week. Why are there no weekend clubs?
— Survey respondent
Growing Our Community

YCL issued {{ fmt(data.cards.v2) }} new library cards in FY24, each representing a new connection to community resources. Among non-users in the survey, 17 of 42 cited being too busy — pointing to scheduling and convenience as the primary barriers to broader engagement.

Young adults (19–24) show the most untapped potential: only 15.4% visit weekly, suggesting the transition out of school-age programming leaves a gap we can fill.

Events for 20–30 somethings looking to make friends. More programs for the 18–22 college age range.
— Survey respondents
What You're Asking For Next

430 patrons wrote detailed open-ended responses about what to add or improve. Top strategic priorities:

Lifelong Learning Programs288 selections — 53% of respondents
Library of Things273 selections — 50% of respondents
Makerspace230 selections — 42% of respondents
Meeting Spaces183 selections — 34% of respondents
Career & Workforce Development176 selections — 32% of respondents
Dedicated Teen Areas169 selections — 31% of respondents
Heavy emphasis on Library of Things and Makerspaces. These spaces will encourage creativity and provide community support by making it more accessible.
— Survey respondent
