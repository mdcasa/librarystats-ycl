{# Section 1 #}
You Keep Coming Back

The library recorded {{ fmt(data.gate.v2) }} visits in FY{{ fy2 }} ({{ pct_span(data.gate.pct) }} vs FY{{ fy1 }}). Our survey confirms the pattern: 92.3% of respondents visited in the past year, with teens leading — 57.1% visit every week.

{{ fmt(data.pc_reservations.v2) }} PC reservation sessions show the library remains a critical technology access point. Website use runs 86–93% across all age groups; 50–64% use the YCL app, including 59% of seniors.

Western York County needs another library. It doesn't have to have all the programs… but a location with computers, books, and a hold shelf so people in Hickory Grove don't have to plan their trips based on when they're running to York.
— Survey respondent

It is hard to browse books as the selection is small in person. I do appreciate being able to get them online, but I love spontaneously getting books.

{# Section 3 #}
Learning Together

YCL offered {{ fmt(data.sessions.v2) }} program sessions, drawing {{ fmt(data.attendance.v2) }} participants {{ pct_span(data.attendance.pct) }}. 61.2% of survey respondents attended a signature event — teens and young adults at 71.4%. Summer Learning Challenge (272 selections) and Winter Reading Challenge (218) led attendance.

Lifelong learning programs were the top strategic priority (53% of respondents), but a clear barrier remains: employed adults need programming after 5 p.m. and on Saturdays.

Equity note: Fort Mill event attendance (51.6%) trails Rock Hill (71.5%) by nearly 20 points — a measurable consequence of space constraints at the Fort Mill branch.
The majority of the programs I'm interested in are held during working hours. I'm only in my 40s and work full time but would love to connect with other people — and it's just not possible during the week. Why are there no weekend clubs?
— Survey respondent
{# Section 4 #}
Growing Our Community

YCL issued {{ fmt(data.cards.v2) }} new library cards in FY{{ fy2 }} {{ pct_span(data.cards.pct) }}, each representing a new connection to community resources. Among non-users in the survey, 17 of 42 cited being too busy — pointing to scheduling and convenience as the primary barriers to broader engagement.

Young adults (19–24) show the most untapped potential: only 15.4% visit weekly, suggesting the transition out of school-age programming leaves a gap we can fill.

Events for 20–30 somethings looking to make friends. More programs for the 18–22 college age range.
— Survey respondents
